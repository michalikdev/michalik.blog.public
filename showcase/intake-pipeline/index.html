The intake pipeline, running

Two minutes of the document pipeline doing its job, plus the screens it produces and the console it prints while it works.

In short

  • A showcase is pictures, a recording and a link — the words around them stay short on purpose.
  • The video is a thumbnail and a link, never an embedded player: an embed weighs about a megabyte and reports every reader to YouTube.
  • The console transcript is real text in the page, so it can be copied and searched.

Questions

Why is the video not embedded?

An embedded player weighs roughly a megabyte before anybody presses play, sets cookies, and reports every reader of the page to YouTube whether or not they cared about the video. On a site that asks for consent, that would track the person who refused.

Can I see the code?

The repository link is on the tool's own page. A showcase is deliberately the shortest document on the site: it shows the thing working and gets out of the way, and the detail lives where somebody went looking for it.

What am I looking at in the recording?

One document going in and a structured record coming out, with the fields it refused to read listed separately. The refusals are the interesting part — a pipeline that never refuses anything is guessing.

Is this running on your own hardware?

Yes, and that is the point of it. Nothing in this pipeline sends a document anywhere; the models it uses run on a machine in the same room, which is the only arrangement that makes it usable for work that cannot leave a building.
